# Decision Rules

## Verdicts

### Can copy

Use when:

- the scene represents a recognizable audience, life state, or desire;
- the product naturally completes that scene;
- the angle clearly reveals, proves, compares, or dramatizes the product;
- all components address the same audience;
- the combination can be reproduced with available people, space, styling, light, and props.

Without performance data, label this as a content-logic judgment or testable combination rather than a proven viral result.

### Replace one component, then test

Use when two components are strong and one is missing or conflicting. Name the exact replacement:

- keep scene and angle, replace product;
- keep product and angle, rebuild scene;
- keep scene and product, replace angle.

Do not recommend vague “optimization.”

### Do not copy

Use when the premise depends on borrowed brand attention, unclear product focus, fake interaction, incompatible components, or production conditions that cannot be reproduced.

## Operator self-entertainment

Flag operator self-entertainment when several of these appear:

- the caption claims a scene that the picture never shows;
- a generic “which one” question has too many unnumbered options;
- a transition creates a reveal but the result has no second-layer value;
- props compete with the product;
- many fashionable elements are assembled without one audience or desire;
- product quantity substitutes for meaningful comparison;
- the scene proves only that the team completed a creative idea;
- viewers cannot imagine ownership, use, identity, occasion, or outcome;
- unrelated brand packaging supplies most of the attention;
- the product could be removed without changing the scene's meaning.

Use this principle:

> A question is not automatically interaction. A transition is not automatically a hook. Many products are not automatically rich choice.

## Scene tests

A valid viral scene should answer:

- Who is this person?
- What kind of life, occasion, identity, or emotional state is shown?
- Which visible details prove that meaning?
- Why does the target viewer want to enter or recognize this scene?

Examples of scene evidence include housing, sunlight, furniture, clothing materials, manicure, hairstyle, accessories, restaurant or party context, motherhood, friendship, getting ready, gifting, or daily routines.

Do not label a plain tabletop as a scene merely because props are placed on it.

## Product tests

Determine whether the product:

- resolves or completes the scene;
- expresses identity, memory, status, beauty, convenience, or occasion;
- remains the first visual focus;
- can be understood without invented features;
- matches the audience implied by styling and environment.

If jewelry supports manicure content or manicure supports jewelry content, identify which is primary from framing, duration, focus, and the final reveal.

## Angle tests

A reusable viral angle should do at least one:

- hide then reveal;
- show before and after;
- prove sparkle, color shift, fit, scale, movement, or use;
- move from detail to full try-on;
- create a real A/B choice;
- use an audience-familiar object as a compatible reveal device;
- make the viewer wait for an outcome.

Describe the physical recipe precisely, such as:

- closed fist → open palm → sunlight close-up;
- macro adjustment → both hands → face-and-product frame;
- cold hair straightener opens → chain appears → pendant appears;
- two hands overlap and rotate → cat-eye light band moves.

## Interaction tests

Interaction is real only when:

- options are few and distinguishable;
- viewers can answer cheaply, usually by letter, number, color, or clear name;
- the choice relates to identity, occasion, or preference;
- the video does not predetermine a meaningless answer.

## Replication tests

Before saying “Can copy,” name the non-negotiable conditions:

- person or hand quality;
- manicure, clothing, and accessories;
- location and lighting;
- product variant and visual behavior;
- prop safety;
- framing and movement.

For tools used only as props, require safe use. For example, place jewelry in a hair straightener only when it is unplugged and fully cold.

## Corrections from user training

- Analyze each new video independently as a new category.
- Prioritize reusable, previously successful scenes and angles over original operational creativity.
- Build a combinatorial library: 10 scenes × 10 angles × a compatible product yields 100 testable materials.
- Save components, not whole-video imitation.
- Reject decorative complexity when it does not strengthen scene-product-angle compatibility.
